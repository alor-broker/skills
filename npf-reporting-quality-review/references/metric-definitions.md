# Metric definitions

Before using a metric, record its formula, numerator, denominator, period, frequency, annualization, currency, units, gross or net basis, valuation date, cash-flow treatment, and source methodology.

Pay particular attention to:

- assets under management versus net assets, pension assets, or accounting balance-sheet assets;
- contributions, inflows, net flows, investment income, and total change in assets;
- accounting profit, comprehensive income, distributable result, and cash flow;
- period return, annualized return, time-weighted return, money-weighted return, nominal return, and real return;
- current yield, yield to maturity, yield to offer, spread, duration, and modified duration;
- volatility, VaR, expected shortfall, drawdown, and stress loss;
- gross, net-of-manager-fee, net-of-fund-expense, and participant-crediting returns.

If the available source does not define a decisive metric, do not supply a conventional definition silently. State the ambiguity and test how it could change the conclusion.
